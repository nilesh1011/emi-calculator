Personal Loan vs Home Loan for Home Renovation — Which is Better in India 2026?

Published on EMI Check  •  7 min read

Planning a kitchen remodel, adding a room, or redoing your bathroom? Home renovation is one of the most common reasons Indian borrowers take a loan. Two options come up most often: a personal loan or a home loan top-up. Both can fund your renovation, but they cost very differently. Choosing the wrong one can mean paying lakhs more in interest than you needed to.

This guide compares both options head to head — interest rates, EMI on a ₹10 lakh renovation loan, tax benefits, and the situations where each makes sense.

Tax Benefits: A Clear Win for Home Loan Top-Up

This is where the home loan top-up has a decisive advantage. Under the Income Tax Act, interest paid on a home loan (including top-up loans used for renovation) qualifies for deduction under Section 24(b):

  • Home Loan Top-Up (renovation): Deduction on interest up to ₹2 lakh per year for a self-occupied property under Section 24(b)
  • Personal Loan: Interest deduction is limited to ₹30,000 per year under Section 24(b) only if you can document that the funds were used exclusively for renovation — this cap applies to all non-home-loan borrowings on a self-occupied property

For someone in the 30% tax bracket, the Section 24(b) deduction on a home loan top-up can mean a tax saving of up to ₹60,000 per year. Over a 5-year loan this adds ₹3 lakh in effective savings on top of the lower interest rate.

Important: To claim tax benefits on a personal loan used for renovation, you must maintain clear documentation — contractor invoices, bank statements showing fund usage, and completion certificates. Without this, the deduction claim can be disallowed. Consult a CA for your specific case.

When to Choose Each Option

Choose Personal Loan When:

  • You do not have an existing home loan
  • You need money within 24–48 hours
  • Renovation cost is under ₹3–5 lakh
  • You plan to repay within 2–3 years
  • Your property has no remaining equity
  • You want minimal paperwork

Choose Home Loan Top-Up When:

  • You already have an active home loan
  • Renovation budget is ₹5 lakh or more
  • You want the lowest possible interest rate
  • You want to claim income tax deduction
  • You can wait 3–7 days for processing
  • You prefer a longer, lower-EMI repayment

What Is a Home Loan Top-Up?

A top-up loan is additional financing offered by your existing home loan lender over and above your current outstanding balance. Because the lender already holds your property documents and knows your repayment history, the process is faster and simpler than taking a fresh secured loan. The interest rate is typically close to your home loan rate — far below what any personal loan charges.

Eligibility depends on your current loan-to-value (LTV) ratio. Most lenders allow a top-up if your outstanding principal is below 75–80% of the property's current market value. If you have been repaying your home loan for 3 or more years, you likely have sufficient equity to qualify.

Quick Rule of Thumb:

Already have a home loan? Always ask your bank about a top-up loan before applying for a personal loan. The rate difference of 4–6% on a renovation amount can easily save ₹1–3 lakh over the repayment period.

No existing home loan? A personal loan is the straightforward choice — compare rates across 3–4 lenders before accepting an offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a personal loan for home renovation in India?

Yes, you can use a personal loan for home renovation in India. Personal loans are unsecured and can be used for any purpose. However, the interest rate is significantly higher at 11–24% compared to 8.5–10.5% for a home loan top-up. If you already have a home loan, a top-up is almost always the cheaper option for renovation funding.

Is a home loan top-up better than a personal loan for home renovation?

For most borrowers with an existing home loan, yes. A top-up loan carries an interest rate 4–6 percentage points lower than a personal loan. On a ₹10 lakh renovation over 5 years this saves over ₹1.36 lakh in total interest, plus potential tax savings under Section 24(b). The only trade-off is slightly longer processing time and the need for sufficient equity in your property.

Is personal loan interest tax deductible for home renovation in India?

A personal loan used specifically for home renovation may qualify for an interest deduction under Section 24(b) of the Income Tax Act, but the limit is ₹30,000 per year for self-occupied property — significantly lower than the ₹2 lakh deduction available on home loan top-ups used for the same purpose. Clear documentation linking the loan to renovation work is mandatory. Consult a tax advisor to confirm eligibility for your situation.

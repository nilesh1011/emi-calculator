How to Calculate EMI for a Home Loan in India — Step by Step

Published on EMI Check  •  5 min read

Buying a home is one of the biggest financial decisions of your life. For most Indians, this means taking a home loan — and the first question everyone asks is: how much will my home loan EMI be? Understanding how to calculate your home loan EMI gives you a clear picture of your monthly commitment before you sign any papers.

What Exactly Is a Home Loan EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay to your bank or lender every month until your loan is fully repaid. Each EMI payment has two parts — one portion goes towards repaying the principal (the original loan amount) and the other covers the interest charged by the lender.

In the early months of a loan, a larger share of your EMI goes towards interest. As time passes, the interest portion reduces and the principal repayment portion increases. This is called the reducing balance method, and it is the standard method used by all banks in India.

The Home Loan EMI Formula

The mathematical formula used to calculate home loan EMI is:

EMI = P × R × (1 + R)ᴾ / ((1 + R)ᴾ − 1)

P = Principal loan amount
R = Monthly interest rate = Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
N = Total number of monthly instalments = Tenure in years × 12

This formula looks complicated, but in practice you never need to calculate it by hand. You can use a free EMI calculator to get an instant result by entering three values.

The Three Factors That Decide Your EMI

1. Loan Amount (Principal)

This is the amount you borrow from the bank. It is usually the property value minus your down payment. Most banks in India finance up to 75–90% of the property value. A higher loan amount directly means a higher EMI.

2. Interest Rate

This is the annual rate at which the bank charges interest on your outstanding loan. Home loan rates in India typically range from 8% to 10.5% depending on the bank, your credit score, and whether you choose a fixed or floating rate. Even a small difference in interest rate can significantly change your EMI and total interest paid over the life of the loan.

3. Loan Tenure

This is the number of years you take to repay the loan. Home loans in India can have tenures of up to 30 years. A longer tenure reduces your monthly EMI but increases the total interest you pay. A shorter tenure means a higher EMI but much lower total interest.

A Practical Example

Let us say Ramesh wants to buy a flat worth ₹60 lakhs. He makes a down payment of ₹10 lakhs and takes a home loan of ₹50 lakhs at 8.75% interest for 20 years. Here is what his EMI looks like:

Loan Amount: ₹50,00,000

Interest Rate: 8.75% per annum

Tenure: 20 years (240 months)

Monthly EMI: approximately ₹44,145

Total Interest Paid: approximately ₹55,94,800

Total Amount Paid: approximately ₹1,05,94,800

Notice that Ramesh ends up paying more in interest than his original loan amount. This is completely normal for long-tenure home loans, and it is exactly why it is important to calculate and plan before borrowing.

Quick Tips Before You Take a Home Loan

  • Always compare at least 3–4 lenders before deciding — even a 0.25% difference in rate saves lakhs over 20 years
  • Check your credit score before applying — a score above 750 usually gets you better rates
  • Make the maximum down payment you can afford — it reduces your loan amount and total interest
  • Consider making part-prepayments whenever you get a bonus or windfall — this reduces the principal faster
  • Do not stretch the tenure just to lower EMI — calculate the total cost first
